Best Starting Words for 5-Letter Word Games

By Jayant Singh · Published 25 October 2026

Every 5-letter word puzzle begins the same way: six empty rows and a blinking cursor. A good first guess is not magic, but it does take a lot of the luck out of the opening. This guide shows how to pick one, using counts we ran on the 12,599-word list that powers Wordsy and on the 576-word list of daily answers.

Which letters show up most?

The table shows how many words contain each letter at least once. The first column of percentages covers every word Wordsy accepts as a guess; the second covers only the 576 words that can be the daily answer.

Two things stand out. First, S looks unusually strong in the big list because so many entries are plurals: 35% of the accepted words end in S, but only 2% of the daily answers do. Second, vowels dominate: A, E, O and I all sit near the top, which is why most strong openers carry two or three of them.

Where the letters sit

Position matters as much as presence, because a green square is worth more than a yellow one. These are the most common letters in each spot across the accepted words:

The fifth place is dominated by S, E and Y, and the first place leans on S, B, C and T. The second place is mostly a vowel, A and O in particular. A word like SLATE or CRANE puts common letters where they tend to live, which is part of why they work.

The differences are small. The best openers share between one and two letters with a typical answer and touch at least one letter of nearly every word. Vowel-heavy words such as ADIEU and AUDIO sit at the bottom of this table because they spend a whole guess on vowels and say little about the consonants that carry the word.

What makes a good first word

  • Five different letters. A repeated letter wastes a square. About a third of 5-letter words repeat a letter, so you will meet doubles later, but they make poor openers.
  • Common letters. Pick from A, E, I, O, R, S, T, L, N. Rare letters (Q, Z, X, J) together appear in only 8% of the words, so guessing them first almost always returns grey.
  • A mix of vowels and consonants. Two vowels and three consonants is a good default.
  • A real, valid word. It can win by luck, and it keeps you honest about spelling.

Your second guess

If the first guess gives you nothing, do not repeat its letters. Choose a word with five new common letters. After CRANE returns all grey, MOIST or GUILT are good follow-ups, because neither shares a letter with CRANE yet both test several of the next most common letters. If your first guess gave you a few yellow or green squares, use the second guess to move yellow letters into new positions and to test one or two fresh letters at the same time.

Should you always use the same opener?

Many players do, and there is a real benefit: after a few weeks you recognise how patterns around your opener usually resolve. Others rotate between two or three words to keep the game fresh. Neither is wrong. What matters is that you spend your first guess finding information, not trying to win with it.
