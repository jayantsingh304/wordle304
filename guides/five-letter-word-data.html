What the Letters in 5-Letter Words Tell You

By Jayant Singh · Published 6 November 2026

Wordsy accepts 12,598 five-letter words as guesses, but only 576 of them are ever the daily answer. That gap matters. Most of the accepted list is made of plurals, past tenses and rarer words that players type but that never come up as a daily puzzle. This guide counts the letters in both lists so you can see how they differ, and what that means for your guesses.

All figures below come from the word lists themselves. Percentages are rounded to one decimal place.

Endings: the biggest difference

The clearest difference is the last letter. In the accepted list, 35.3% of words end in S (4,447 words). In the daily answers, only 2.1% (12 words) end in S. Plurals are easy to type and count as valid guesses, but they almost never win.

The answers favour E and T at the end, so when a word fits a pattern like _ _ _ _E or _ _ _ _T, it is a more likely answer than one ending in S.

Starting letters

Among the daily answers, the most common first letters are S (14.8%, 85 words), C (10.2%, 59 words), B (8.3%, 48 words) and A (7.8%, 45 words). P and T follow. Starting letters matter less than endings, but a game where you have already ruled out the common starters helps you narrow the list.

Vowels

Most five-letter words have two vowels. In the accepted list, 57% have exactly two vowels (A, E, I, O, U), and 32% have one. Among the daily answers the picture is more varied:

So in the answers, a word with two vowels is the most likely shape, and a word with three or more is unusual. That is why vowel-heavy openers spend a guess on information you may already expect.

Double letters

About a third of accepted words (35.4%) repeat a letter. Among the daily answers the share is lower, at 25.9%, so roughly one answer in four has a double letter. When you see a repeated letter in a clue, remember that the word may be one of the 74% that use five different letters.

For how Wordsy scores a repeated letter, see Repeated Letters Explained.

Endings that rarely appear

Two endings show the gap between the lists clearly:

  • -ED: 1.2% of accepted words, but only 0.3% of daily answers (2 words).
  • -ER: 3.3% of accepted words, and 6.6% of daily answers.

An answer ending in -ER is about twice as likely as the accepted list would suggest. An answer ending in -ED is rare.

What to do with these numbers

  • Don't spend your opening guess on S. It is in half the accepted words, but almost never at the end of an answer.
  • Test endings with E, T and R once you have a few greens or yellows, because those are the common last letters in the answers.
  • Use the vowel count. If your first guess shows only one vowel in the answer, look for a word with a second vowel, which is the most common shape.
  • Expect a double letter about one time in four. If your word has none, it is not a reason to change your plan.

How the numbers were counted

We read the two word lists that Wordsy uses: the list of accepted guesses and the list of 576 daily answers. Each word was counted once, and only five-letter alphabetic words were included. Vowels are A, E, I, O and U. The lists change rarely, so these figures will be updated if they do.
