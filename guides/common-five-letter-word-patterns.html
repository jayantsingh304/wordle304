Common 5-Letter Word Patterns: Endings, Families and Double Letters

By Jayant Singh · Published 25 October 2026

Solving 5-letter puzzles gets easier once you stop thinking in single letters and start thinking in chunks. Words cluster around a small number of endings, starting pairs, vowel shapes and families. This guide shows the patterns that appear most in the 12,599-word list behind Wordsy and in its 576 daily answers.

Plurals ending in -S take up many of the places in the accepted list, which is why S is such a strong fifth letter. The daily answers are mostly everyday singular words, so endings like -ER (AFTER, ALTER, EAGER, ELDER) and -LY (DAILY, EARLY, SILLY, REPLY) are more useful there.

Consonant pairs such as ST-, CH-, TR- and BL- are everywhere, so if your first guess puts a T and an S in yellow, try ST- at the start before anything exotic.

Most words have two vowels, a large minority have just one, and three-vowel words such as ABOUT, AGAIN and ALIVE are rare enough that a guess with four vowels is seldom worth it. If a word seems to have no vowel at all, remember Y: words like GYPSY, LYNCH and RHYME use it instead.

Word families that trip people up

A family is a group of words that share everything except one letter. They are the main reason a solver with four letters placed still loses. These come from the daily answer list:

When you meet a family, stop guessing candidates one by one. Use a test word that covers several of the missing letters, as shown in our strategy guide.

Double letters

Double letters are common: about 35% of the accepted words repeat a letter. Examples from the daily list include AGREE, ALLOW, APPLE, APPLY, ARRAY, ARROW, ASSET, BERRY, BLESS, BLOOD, BLOOM, BOOST. Letters most often doubled are L, E, S, O, R and T. Read how repeated letters are scored so you can use them as a clue.

A pattern checklist

  1. Ask what the last letter probably is: S, E and Y lead.
  2. Ask whether a common ending (-ER, -LY, -ED) could fit the greens you have.
  3. Ask whether a common start (ST-, CH-, TR-, BL-) could fit the yellows.
  4. Count the vowels: if you found two, the rest are probably consonants.
  5. Check for a family and, if so, use a test word.
