Repeated Letters Explained: Why Your Duplicate Letter Turned Grey

By Jayant Singh · Published 25 October 2026

You type a word with two Ls, one turns yellow and the other grey, and you wonder whether the game made a mistake. It did not. Repeated letters follow a precise rule, and once you know it, a grey duplicate becomes useful information instead of a puzzle in itself.

The rule

Every letter in the answer can be matched only as many times as it actually appears. The game scores a guess in two passes:

  1. Greens first. Each letter in exactly the right spot turns green and uses up one copy of that letter in the answer.
  2. Yellows next. Going left to right, each remaining letter that is still available in the answer turns yellow and uses up another copy. Once all copies are used, any further copy of that letter turns grey.

So a grey square does not always mean "this letter is not in the word". It can mean "this letter is in the word, but you already have all the copies that exist".

  • LLAMA vs HELLO. HELLO has two Ls. Neither L in LLAMA is in the right spot, so both take a copy and turn yellow. The A and M are not in HELLO, so they turn grey.
  • SPILL vs HELLO. The fourth letter, L, is in the right place, so it turns green and uses one of the two Ls. The fifth letter is also an L, and there is one copy left, so it turns yellow.
  • GEESE vs EERIE. EERIE has three Es. The Es in places 2 and 5 are in the right spot, so they turn green. The E in place 3 is not, but one copy of E is still unclaimed, so it turns yellow. All three Es are coloured, which tells you the answer has at least three.
  • EERIE vs GEESE. Swap the words and the pattern changes. GEESE also has three Es (places 2, 3 and 5). The Es in places 2 and 5 of your guess are green, and the first E claims the remaining copy, so it is yellow.
  • BOOKS vs ROBOT. ROBOT has two Os and one B. The first O in BOOKS is not in the right place but the second is, so the second O is green. The B is in the word but not first, so it is yellow, and the other O takes the remaining copy, so it is yellow too.

What a grey duplicate tells you

If a letter appears twice in your guess and one copy is green or yellow while the other is grey, you have learned the exact number of that letter in the answer: one. If both copies are coloured, the answer has at least two. This is among the most useful clues in the game and it is easy to miss.

How often does it matter?

About 35% of the 5-letter words in the Wordsy list repeat at least one letter, including many of the most common ones such as AGREE, ALLOW, APPLE, ARROW and ASSET. Ignoring doubles is one of the most common reasons a solver runs out of guesses with four letters already placed.

Tips

  • If your guess has a repeated letter and one copy turns grey, do not use that letter a second time in later guesses.
  • If you are stuck with four known letters and no word fits, try a word that repeats one of them.
  • Do not open with a word that repeats a letter. You learn more from five different letters. See best starting words.
