Five-Letter Word Strategy: How to Solve in Fewer Guesses

By Jayant Singh · Published 25 October 2026

Six guesses is plenty for a 5-letter word, but only if every guess teaches you something. These are the habits that separate a steady solver from someone who relies on luck.

1. Treat the first two guesses as exploration

Use guesses one and two to discover which of the common letters are in the word. A reasonable plan is: guesses 1 and 2 find letters, guesses 3 and 4 fix their positions, guesses 5 and 6 solve. If you get lucky early, great; if not, you are still on schedule. Our guide to starting words has data on good openers.

2. Lock in greens, rotate yellows

A green square is permanent: keep that letter in that spot for every later guess. A yellow square tells you two things at once: the letter is in the word, and it is not where you put it. Move it to a new position each time. Players who re-use the same yellow position are the ones who run out of tries.

3. Watch for word families

The biggest trap in 5-letter puzzles is a family of words that differ by one letter. Take this game, where the answer is GRAPE:

SLATE
CRANE
GRADE
GRAPE

SLATE finds the A and E in their correct places. CRANE confirms the R and gives _RA_E, which fits GRAPE, GRADE, GRACE, BRAVE, DRAPE and several more. At that point the game is no longer about discovering letters but about choosing the right candidate. Guessing a real candidate such as GRADE gets you three greens and leaves a single missing letter.

4. Break a family with a test word

Sometimes the family is long. Suppose you know the word is _IGHT and only two guesses are left. The candidates include EIGHT, FIGHT, LIGHT, NIGHT, RIGHT, SIGHT, TIGHT. Guessing them one at a time could cost you the game. Instead, play a word that is not a candidate but contains several of the possible first letters. FRESH works well: its F, R and S each test one candidate and its E tests another. These are the seven patterns it produces:

FRESH splits the seven words into 5 different patterns, and only LIGHT, NIGHT, TIGHT share one. That is far better than guessing, because after one guess you are down to a single candidate or a pair, which turns a coin flip over several words into a near-certain win. It costs you the small chance of winning that turn, and it is usually worth it.

5. Remember how words end

In the 12,599 accepted words, 35% end in S, about 10% in E and 10% in Y. If you are stuck on the last letter, those three are the best bets. Common endings such as -ER, -LY and -ED can fix two letters at once. See common word patterns for more.

6. Do not forget doubles

About 35% of 5-letter words repeat a letter, such as AGREE, APPLE and ARROW. If you have four distinct letters placed and nothing fits, a double is probably hiding. Read how repeated letters are scored so a grey square never fools you.

7. Use a hint when it saves a guess

Wordsy gives you hints: type hint or tap the 💡 button and the game reveals one letter in its correct spot. The best time to use one is when you have two or three guesses left and several candidates remain, because a revealed letter often cuts the list to one or two words. Using a hint on guess two usually wastes it.

A simple checklist

  1. Open with five different common letters.
  2. Second guess: five new letters, unless the first gave you a lot.
  3. From guess three, every guess should be a word that could be the answer, unless a test word breaks a family.
  4. Re-read the board before each guess: greens fixed, yellows moved, greys ruled out.
  5. With two guesses left, stop gambling and take the hint or the test word.
